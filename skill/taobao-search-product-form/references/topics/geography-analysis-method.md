# Listing-location analysis method

Source classes: U for the user-confirmed research question and staged workflow; A for this diagnostic method; E for the observed input field and verifiable script results. Apply the method to each search export without carrying over one search term's region names, counts or opportunity conclusion.

## Evidence unit and meaning

The cleaned natural sheet supplies one first-seen row per product ID. Its `地址` is a listing-export location label. It may describe display, shipment or another platform address convention; this dataset alone cannot establish the actual dispatch warehouse, merchant registration, factory, sourcing channel or production cluster. Preserve the raw address alongside parsed province and city. Standardize whitespace and municipalities; retain unknown and unparsed values instead of guessing a city.

Use three simultaneous units: unique natural product ID, exact shop name, and exact shop–address pair. A shop may appear in several addresses, so regional shop counts cannot be added into a global shop total. Identify each region's top-shop product share and within-region listing HHI to test whether an apparent cluster is broad or dominated by a few merchants. These are search-listing concentration measures, not platform market shares.

## Ordered analysis questions

1. **Where are the visible listings?** Count natural products, distinct shops, shop–address pairs and missing or unparsed addresses by province and city/address. Show product share, price quartiles, displayed `付款人数` median and high-payment product count. A larger listing count signals visible supply in this search snapshot, not manufacturing origin or total market scale.
2. **What do those listings claim to be?** If a title-root artifact from the exact same cleaned workbook exists, join by product ID after verifying the workbook hash and product-title population. Cross region with materially informative root facets; keep the full root–region grid in machine output. For each cell calculate distinct products, distinct shops, within-region coverage, share of the root's products located there, and location quotient (within-region root coverage divided by corpus root coverage). Require at least ten products and three shops before treating a city–root cell as a narrative screening lead; this guard is not statistical significance. Compare the cell's top-shop share to avoid calling one seller a regional specialty. Title roots remain seller language; physical forms require image review.
3. **What is the visible price and payment pattern?** Use the natural-position price and source `付款人数`, show medians and an all-sample 90th-percentile payment threshold. The source does not specify the payment period. For regional price comparisons, compare within the same title-root proposition and similar price quartile band where sample size permits. Product mixes, rank position and brand presence may confound differences. Never call payment people units sold, GMV or a causal response to region.
4. **Who appears in advertising positions?** Count advertising appearances, unique advertised product IDs and exact shops separately by their observed address. Record product-ID overlap with natural positions and address differences for the same ID. Repeated ad appearances are not unique products, spend or impressions; ad-only products do not enter natural price/payment counts. Advertising geography adds competitive context, not a purchase or production signal.
5. **What should be investigated next?** Name contrasting, evidence-backed regional cases: broad merchant participation versus single-shop concentration; title-root specialization versus generic products; meaningful natural-payment records versus listings with little visible traction; advertising-heavy versus naturally represented regions. For white-label selection, turn these into image-review and supplier-verification leads rather than a final region or product decision.

It writes `geography_detail.json` (product, ad appearance, shop–address and joined root evidence) and `geography_summary.json` (region, root–region, ad-region statistics and definitions). Write a separate Markdown conclusion tracing named facts to those outputs. Register all three artifacts under `classify/geography` in the development ledger.

Reconcile natural IDs and row counts to the clean sheet; verify each region's product count and shop count with independent grouping; inspect multi-address shops and blank/unparsed addresses; verify the optional root hash and product-title join; keep advertising appearances separate from distinct ad products; and sample named conclusion figures against the JSON. If the root artifact is unavailable, complete the standalone geographic analysis and explicitly leave root–region findings unavailable. Reject a mismatched root artifact rather than joining unrelated runs. Preserve approved upstream stages.
